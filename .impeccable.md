## Design Context

### Users
The first user is a technical individual running meetings on an Apple Silicon Mac. They want to stay present during calls, retain an accurate private record, and turn that record into useful follow-up without operating a complicated recording studio. The core job is to start confidently, see that capture is healthy, trust the live transcript, and leave with evidence-backed notes.

### Brand Personality
Calm, precise, private. The interface should reduce recording anxiety and feel dependable during long, high-attention meetings. Copy is direct and humane, with no breathless AI language.

### Aesthetic Direction
A quiet editorial utility canvas inspired by a well-made field recorder and a carefully annotated notebook. The application owns its neutral surfaces in both light and dark appearances; it does not inherit system-blue selection, glass-heavy sidebar chrome, or generic dashboard cards. Use warm paper neutrals, ink-like UI typography, moss/ink navigation selection, and one vermilion recording accent. Serif is reserved for sustained transcript, note, and summary reading—not for controls or application navigation. Avoid neon AI gradients, shadows as hierarchy, uniform card grids, oversized metrics, and generic chatbot styling.

The product should remain calm and private on every supported window size. Preserve one information architecture while adapting its canvas to the available detail container: compact below 760 points, regular through 1099, and wide from 1100. Keep generous but purposeful gutters (24/32/48–64), center utility content around a 1240-point maximum, and hold reading passages to roughly 680–720 points. Use a right rail only where the detail container is genuinely wide enough.

### Design Principles
- Make recording state unmistakable through color, motion, wording, and menu-bar presence.
- Keep the active conversation visually dominant; advanced model and provider settings stay progressively disclosed.
- Treat timestamps and evidence as first-class navigation, not decorative metadata.
- Use space and typography for hierarchy; containers appear only when they clarify interaction.
- Build app-owned Button, Field, Toggle, and segmented treatments around native SwiftUI semantics rather than replacing accessible controls with decorative gestures.
- Preserve platform-native accessibility, keyboard control, focus, reduced motion, and semantic colors.
